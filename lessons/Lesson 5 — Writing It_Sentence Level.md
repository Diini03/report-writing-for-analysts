# Lesson 5 — Writing It: Sentence Level

## Why this lesson matters

You now know the structure (Lesson 3) and where it lives in Power BI (Lesson 4). The last gap is the sentence itself. Analysts with the right structure still lose credibility by writing vague, unconfident, or unsupported sentences. This lesson is about fixing that at the line level.

## The four most common sentence mistakes

### Mistake 1: Vague claims with no number

**Weak:** "Sales dropped a lot in the East region."
**Strong:** "East region sales dropped 18% quarter-over-quarter, from $210K to $172K."

A number turns an opinion into a fact. If you can't attach a number, you probably haven't finished the analysis yet — go back and find it before writing the sentence.

### Mistake 2: Hedging that removes all confidence

**Weak:** "It seems like maybe delivery times could possibly be a factor."
**Strong:** "Delivery times increased from 4.2 to 7.6 days over the same period — the timing lines up closely with the sales drop."

You're allowed to be uncertain (that's what the Limitations section is for), but don't bury a real finding under five hedge words. State what the data shows, then note the uncertainty separately and clearly.

### Mistake 3: Describing instead of explaining

**Weak:** "The chart shows churn increased in Q3."
**Strong:** "Churn increased in Q3, concentrated among Basic-plan users inactive for 14+ days — pointing to disengagement rather than price as the driver."

"The chart shows X" is a description of a picture. Nobody needs you to narrate a chart they can already see. Your job is the second half of that sentence — the part the chart _can't_ say on its own.

### Mistake 4: Recommendations with no owner or action

**Weak:** "We should look into improving the shipping process."
**Strong:** "Recommend the operations team audit the East region's shipping vendor contract before Q4 peak season."

"Look into" is not an action — it's a postponement. A real recommendation names what should happen, and ideally who should do it.

## Side-by-side: full paragraph rewrite

**Weak paragraph (real style beginners write):**

> "Looking at the data, it seems like the West region did pretty well this quarter, while East and Central maybe struggled a bit. This could be due to a few different reasons. We might want to look into this more."

**Strong rewrite:**

> "West region exceeded target by 8% this quarter. East and Central both missed target by over 10%, concentrated in the furniture category. The pattern is consistent with a fulfillment issue introduced by East's new shipping vendor in July, rather than a drop in demand. Recommend renegotiating the vendor contract before Q4."

Same underlying facts. The difference is entirely in confidence, numbers, and a clear ending.

## A rewriting drill — try it yourself

Rewrite each weak sentence below using what you just learned. There's no single correct answer — the goal is: add a number, remove the hedge, and/or turn description into explanation.

1. "The dashboard shows that customer complaints went up."
2. "Revenue in the North region seems kind of flat compared to last year."
3. "We might need to think about doing something about the delays."

## Quick check

1. Pick one sentence you've written in a past project's README, comment, or notes. Rewrite it using the four rules above.
2. Why does "the chart shows X" fail as a report sentence, even if X is true?
3. What's the one thing every strong recommendation sentence needs that a weak one is missing?

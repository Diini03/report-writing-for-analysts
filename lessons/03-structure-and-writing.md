# Lesson 3: How to Structure and Write One

## The skeleton

Six parts, always in this order.

| Part           | What goes in it                                |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Title          | Topic and time period, one line                |
| Summary        | 2 to 4 sentences, written last                 |
| Key findings   | 2 to 4 points, every one with a number         |
| Explanation    | Why it's happening, what connects the findings |
| Recommendation | A specific action, owner if possible           |
| Limitations    | What the data can't tell you                   |

## Walking through it, one part at a time

**Title.** One line. _Q3 Regional Sales Review, July to September._

**Summary.** Written last, after everything else exists, because it has to summarize real work, not a guess.

> West region beat target by eight percent. East and central both missed target by more than ten percent. The shortfall traces back to a new shipping vendor causing delivery delays. Recommend renegotiating the contract before Q4.

**Your turn.** Below are three raw findings. Write a two sentence summary for them.

> East region furniture sales down 18 percent year over year. Delivery time up from 4.2 to 7.6 days. Complaint tickets tagged late delivery up 340 percent.

**Key findings.** Every point needs a number attached to it. A finding with no number is an opinion wearing a bullet point.

> - Furniture revenue down 18 percent year over year in the east
> - Average delivery time up from 4.2 to 7.6 days
> - Complaint tickets tagged late delivery up 340 percent

**Explanation.** The part most beginners skip. This is where you show you actually understood the data, not just described it.

> The drop lines up with a new shipping vendor onboarded in July. Demand itself is flat, so the problem is fulfillment, not interest.

**Your turn.** Given these findings, write one explanation sentence: signups are flat, but time to first purchase has grown from 2 days to 9 days.

**Recommendation.** Specific, with an action, an owner if you can manage it.

> Recommend operations renegotiate the east region shipping contract before Q4 peak season.

**Limitations.** One or two honest lines, not a formality.

> This covers east and central only, west was excluded since it hit target. Delivery times are self reported by the vendor, not independently checked.

## Full example, start to finish

**Title:** Titanic Survival Analysis, Evacuation Policy Implications

> **Summary.** Survival correlated far more with cabin location and gender than with fare paid. Third class passengers were nearly three times more likely to die than first class, mainly due to distance from lifeboats. Recommend any modern evacuation protocol prioritize physical proximity to exits over passenger category.
>
> **Key findings.** First class survival close to 63 percent, third class close to 24 percent. Female survival close to 74 percent against male at 19 percent. Survival drops sharply for lower deck cabins regardless of class.
>
> **Explanation.** Fare alone doesn't explain the gap. Grouping by cabin shows class was acting as a proxy for distance to the lifeboats, so the real cause is structural, not about who was prioritized.
>
> **Recommendation.** Design evacuation protocols around proximity to exits, not passenger category.
>
> **Limitations.** Cabin data is missing for about 77 percent of passengers. Treat the cabin finding as directional, not conclusive.

## The sentence level habits

Four habits separate strong writing from weak, no matter how good the structure is underneath.

**Attach a number to every claim.**

> Weak: sales dropped a lot in the east.
> Strong: east region sales dropped 18 percent, from 210 to 172 thousand.

**Say what the data shows plainly, save the hedging for the limitations section.**

> Weak: it seems like maybe delivery times could possibly be a factor.
> Strong: delivery times rose from 4.2 to 7.6 days, right in step with the sales drop.

**Explain, don't describe.**

> Weak: the chart shows churn went up in Q3.
> Strong: churn went up in Q3, concentrated among basic plan users who'd gone quiet for two weeks, pointing to boredom rather than price.

**Give every recommendation an action, not a postponement.**

> Weak: we should look into improving shipping.
> Strong: recommend operations audit the east region vendor contract before Q4.

**Your turn.** Rewrite these three the strong way.

1. The dashboard shows that customer complaints went up.
